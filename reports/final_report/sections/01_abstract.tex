\begin{abstract}
Standard vision Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), such as ResMLP and MLP-Mixer, rely on columnar architectures that process fixed-resolution token grids without spatial inductive biases or multi-scale feature hierarchies. Consequently, they require excessive parameters and extensive training data to learn basic visual representations. In this work, we present \textbf{Pyramid-ResMLP}, a hierarchical vision MLP architecture designed to break this parameter-efficiency barrier. Pyramid-ResMLP incorporates an overlapping convolutional stem, a three-stage multi-scale token downsampling pyramid, and \textbf{RepTokenMix}, a structural re-parameterization mechanism that trains with parallel local depthwise convolutions and fuses them into a single linear projection for deployment.

We conduct systematic 60-epoch ablation studies on Tiny-ImageNet-200 ($64 \times 64$ resolution) alongside hardware inference profiling on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU. Our empirical results demonstrate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy gain from local inductive bias:} Adding depthwise convolutional branches during training boosts validation Top-1 accuracy from \textbf{53.75\%} (pure linear baseline) to \textbf{57.22\%} (+3.47\% absolute improvement) and Top-5 accuracy from \textbf{77.66\%} to \textbf{80.49\%}, with negligible parameter overhead during training and zero parameter overhead during deployment.
    \item \textbf{Benefit of overlapping stem:} An overlapping convolutional stem outperforms non-overlapping patch embedding by $+0.10\%$ Top-5 accuracy (\textbf{80.55\%} vs. \textbf{80.45\%}) by smoothing token boundaries and mitigating edge artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Zero-overhead inference:} Hardware profiling on Tesla T4 reveals that re-parameterizing RepTokenMix reduces inference latency from 3.80~ms to \textbf{2.83~ms} at batch size 1 (a \textbf{1.34$\times$ speedup}) and increases throughput from 263.1 to \textbf{353.6} images/second. Across all batch sizes (1 to 128), maximum numerical error between training and deployed models remains strictly below $5.96 \times 10^{-7}$, confirming exact mathematical equivalence with zero inference penalty.
\end{enumerate}
Finally, we discuss architectural trade-offs, identify two specific physical limitations regarding fixed token resolution and deploy tensor memory, and outline concrete future directions including local window partitioning and downstream dense prediction tasks.
\end{abstract}
